The big story
IntegrationsIn development

A guest taps Order — and the receipt prints at the bar

Until this cycle, an order placed in the guest app or on the room TV ended its life inside our platform: someone read it off a screen and re-typed it into the hotel's own cash register. This fortnight we connected the two directly. The platform now talks to Elektronček GIS Prestige — the register a large share of Slovenian hospitality runs on — in both directions.

The first half brings the register's world in. Its menu, prices and photos sync on a schedule into a hidden POS import inbox. Staff then pick items out of that inbox into their real guest-facing catalogues. From then on the register keeps the prices and names up to date wherever an item has been placed, while everything the staff decided — where it sits, whether it is visible, how it is presented — is never overwritten. Items that disappear from the register are quietly taken off the menu instead of being left there to be ordered.

The second half is the money chain, and it is the part that matters. The moment a card payment is confirmed, the order is submitted to the hotel's register: the register fiscalises it, prints the kitchen or bar ticket with the room number on it, and deducts the stock. No re-typing, no lost orders at a busy bar, and a legally valid receipt without anyone touching the till.

Chat that puts itself back together

On 30 July the chat looked dead on preprod. It wasn't — three separate faults were making it look dead. All three are fixed: the chat now reconnects on its own when the network comes back, replays anything that arrived while it was down, and tells the guest when the assistant is briefly busy instead of going silent.

You can only book a slot that actually exists

Bookable items — a spa treatment, a table, a tennis court — previously offered the guest a generic list of times, and the hotel found out about the impossible ones afterwards. Managers now define real opening hours, per-date exceptions and how many bookings each slot can take, and the pickers on the TV and in the guest app only ever offer what is genuinely available.

Fast check-in stopped rejecting passports

Guests checking in with a passport were being blocked outright, and the document scanner asked everyone for the "front" of the card — so a passport holder photographed the right page and the system rejected it. Passports are now a first-class document, and switching document type no longer leaves the previous photo behind.

Admin panelIn QA

Hotels can finally manage their own staff

Until this cycle, only Nevron could add, remove or re-role a hotel's staff accounts — user management was reserved for our own superusers, so every new receptionist was a support request. A hotel Admin can now run their own people, inside a set of rules that make it safe.

Admin panelIn QA

Alerts go to the right people — and only them

Every Nevron global admin received every staff alert for every hotel: roughly fifteen people times twenty-seven alert types times every tenant, with no way to change it. Each hotel now decides, per alert category, which roles and which individuals get told.

The number on the key packet is now the number for the app

Guests log into the app with their name plus a four-digit code. At hotels running Hotelinco, that code can now be the guest's actual door PIN — the one already printed on the key packet at the desk — instead of a second, different number nobody explained to them.

The platform learned which email addresses are fake

Guests who book through Booking.com, Airbnb, Expedia or Agoda arrive with a relay address that forwards for a while and then stops. We treated those as real. The platform now recognises them, never offers one back to a guest, and collects a reachable address instead.
