The big story
Guest appStaff appIn development

Both Nevron apps became real phone apps

Until now, the guest app and the crew app have lived in the browser: you got a link, you opened it, and if you were lucky you saved it to your home screen. This fortnight, both became genuine iPhone and Android apps — the kind you install from the App Store and Google Play, with an icon, a branded splash screen, and notifications that reach the phone even when the app is closed.

A large share of the cycle's work went into everything that separates a website from a real app. Push notifications now travel through the phone's own notification system, for guests and for hotel crew alike — and the app quietly re-registers itself when the phone rotates its delivery address, so notifications don't silently stop after a few weeks. Guests can sign in with Continue with Google or Sign in with Apple next to the existing email login. Password autofill, biometric unlock and the phone keyboard now behave the way they do in native apps, because the app finally identifies itself to the phone the way native apps do.

The road to the stores is nearly walked. Developer accounts, signing keys, artwork, privacy declarations and export-compliance paperwork are done for both apps; every build is now produced, signed and handed to test phones automatically, with no developer laptop in the loop; and the staff app's store submission package — reviewer notes, listing copy, demo hotel — is written. A dedicated demo hotel now exists so store reviewers and prospects always have something real to look at. The apps are not in the stores yet — submission is the next step — but for the first time, everything the stores will ask for exists.

Paying from the TV now follows the hotel's rules

Room Care orders placed on the TV used to be charged straight to the room, no questions asked — even at hotels that had configured card payment or a payment choice. The TV now honours the same payment settings as the app, and the TV checkout tells the payment system which stay is paying, so validation happens against the right guest.

An approved late checkout reshuffles housekeeping by itself

When a hotel approved a late checkout, the cleaning schedule didn't hear about it — the room still showed as due for cleaning at the original time. An approved late checkout now shifts the whole schedule for that room, and the skip-cleaning setting stopped offering a nonsensical cap of zero.

IntegrationsIn QA

Shared menus can now be sold by card at register hotels

Last edition's big story connected orders to the hotel's cash register — but items from shared catalogues (one menu offered across several hotels) couldn't ride that chain, so register-connected hotels couldn't sell them by card at all. Shared and manually created items can now carry a register ID, and card orders for them fiscalize like everything else. Prices on shared items also recalculate live when the source hotel changes them.

Under the hood

Invisible, but you'll feel it

PlatformIn QA

Copying a hotel got its time back — and its brain

Duplicating or importing a hotel was crawling: the media copy alone took over eight minutes, and a hidden lookup wasted a further quarter of an hour. Media now copies eight files at a time (over eight minutes down to about two and a half), the wasteful lookup is gone, hotel creation got its own dedicated worker so it can't be stuck behind other background work — and the copied hotel's AI concierge now rebuilds its knowledge, so it answers about the new hotel instead of knowing nothing.

PlatformIn QA

Background jobs sealed against wrong-hotel reads

A platform-wide hardening sweep closed a subtle class of risk: under our production connection pooling, a background job that switches to a hotel's data in one step and reads in the next could — in rare, load-dependent moments — read the wrong hotel. Every such site in emails, syncs and scheduled jobs was reviewed and pinned so the switch and the read happen as one inseparable step, and an automated guard now blocks new code from reintroducing the pattern.
