A prospect can now watch a whole hotel stay — on their own hotel

Selling a hotel platform has always had the same problem: the prospect sees a demo hotel that is not theirs, driven by a salesperson clicking quickly through screens they do not understand. Last edition the platform learned to build a working hotel from a prospect's own website. This fortnight it learned to show that hotel off by itself.

A salesperson can now create a personal demo stay for each named person at the prospect hotel and send them a link. The link signs the person straight into their own demo stay — no codes, no forms. A small invite offers a guided tour, and a pointer that moves like a finger plays one guest's whole stay in five chapters: arriving with fast check-in, asking the AI concierge for food and really ordering the dish it suggests, a room-care request and skip cleaning, an AI-planned day built from the guest's interests with a late check-out, and finally the bill with everything on it. At any point the prospect can say “take over from here” and click around themselves.

Prospect hotels now come out of the website reader demo-ready: a proven home-screen layout, a seeded chef's menu and nearby rental services the AI can find, tour-friendly settings, and up to three interests the hotel is best at, so the tour's day plan fits the place. Global Admin shows who opened their tour and how far they watched.

Guest app

Quicker paths to what a guest actually wants

Guest appAdmin panelIn QA

Late check-out, skip cleaning and friends got their own front-door tiles

My Room holds seven guest actions — late check-out, skip cleaning, room change, room upgrade, both together, extend stay, report an issue. Until now a guest had to open My Room to find any of them. Hotels can now put each action on the home screen as its own tile, and point a banner straight at one — a “Stay longer” banner that opens Extend stay directly. Every hotel also received starter icons for these actions, and Laundry and Ratings got default icons too.

Pop-up messages stopped covering the buttons

The guest app's short messages — “Item added to cart!”, errors, warnings — were bright default pills that sat on top of the header, the AI orb and the chat's quick buttons. They are now one calm card in the hotel's own colours, chosen by the founder on the Design Hub, with a small icon for success, error, warning or info.

A signed-in guest's chat is finally filed under that guest

Every chat in the guest app was being saved as an anonymous website visitor — even when the guest was signed in. Now a signed-in guest's chat belongs to that guest: staff see who they are talking to, and the assistant can use what the hotel knows about them. The old second chat page was retired.

AI concierge

Honest, personal, and aware of the time

Guest appWebsite chatIn QA

The assistant may no longer promise what it cannot deliver

The AI concierge cannot book anything. But a reply such as “your table will be ready at 7” sounds like a booking, and a guest will rely on it. A founder ruling this fortnight made the line explicit: stating the result as settled is a fake booking, in any tense; saying what happens next (“reception will check and confirm”) is honest. The chat rule and the automatic pre-release check both follow it.

Guest appAdmin panelIn QA

A guest's diet and interests now shape the answers

A guest's stated preferences already reached the assistant — and were ignored. A vegetarian asking where to eat got an answer without a word about it, and the “Recommended for you” row hid every dish that mentioned “vegan” from a vegan. Now a hotel can mark an interest as a dietary need; the assistant suggests only what fits, staff see it under Must know, and the recommended row stops hiding the right dishes. When several options fit a question, the assistant also leans toward the guest's ticked interests and says why.

Guest appWebsite chatIn QA

The assistant knows what time it is at the hotel

The assistant used to know only the server's date, in UTC, with no time of day — so “is the spa open now?” had no honest answer, and a guest in Dubai could get the wrong date near midnight. All three chat channels now receive the hotel's own date, time and time zone. A hotel without a time zone gets a careful instruction instead: never state whether something is open right now, ask reception. Hotels built from a website get a time zone from their country.

Admin panel & reports

Seeing how the apps are really used

Admin panelGlobal AdminIn-room TVIn QA

App usage analytics: built end to end, switched off until legal signs off

Hotels and Nevron could see orders and requests, but not how the apps themselves are used. The full pipeline now exists: the guest app, the room TV, the staff app and the admin panel can each report which screens are used, what is tapped and for how long, and Global Admin gets one page showing every hotel's usage and whether its tracking is healthy. Room TVs now report channel viewing by length, film plays and viewing by hour. Collection ships switched off everywhere and stays off until the legal review is closed.

Privacy & safety

Closing doors that should never have been open

Guest appIn QA

Knowing a guest's email is no longer enough to act as them

Two serious gaps were found and closed. At hotels with fast check-in but no PMS connection, anyone who typed a guest's email into the public form got back that guest's personal details and a short pass to act as them. And the guest app's manual sign-in could link a newcomer to an existing guest's stay by email alone, giving access to their orders, bill and chat. Now every no-PMS fast check-in starts a fresh, empty guest, an existing stay is rejoined only when email, room and surname all match, and both doors are rate-limited.

Guest appGlobal AdminIn QA

ID photos can now be deleted automatically after the stay

The app's privacy policy will promise that passport and ID photos are deleted a set number of days after a stay. Until now nothing deleted them. A nightly job now permanently deletes ID images N days after the guest's latest check-out, with N set once for the whole platform in Global Admin. It ships at 0 — off — until a value is chosen. Guests also see a short notice before their ID photo is taken, and the guest iOS app now carries Apple's required privacy declaration.

Under the hood

Bringing older hotels across, and keeping the engine steady

Nevron opsIn development

The road from Nevron Ware to Nevron Core is built

Five hotels on the older Nevron Ware platform — Australian BHP villages and the NGV office — are moving to Nevron Core. The tooling to do it is now in place: an exporter that reads a Ware hotel into a clean package, an importer that recreates rooms, TVs, device groups, apps, TV dashboard, catalogues, signage and weather in Core, a pre-flight report that flags conflicts before anything is written, and a browser demo TV per hotel. Content is written in Australian English.

PR #1058PR #1059PR #1060PR #1061
Nevron opsIn QA

Background jobs, deploys and translations stopped misbehaving

Three platform-wide problems were fixed. About 30 scheduled jobs ran against hotels that were still being created, flooding preprod with errors; they now skip those hotels and never run in the wrong place if a hotel was deleted. Deploys stopped breaking the live app during the window when the database is already updated but the old app is still serving. And messages the server writes itself — push, emails, order statuses — now come in the guest's language at the 267 newer hotels where they had fallen back to English.
